# Understanding Uncertainty — Reading Roadmap (Aug 25 – Oct 1, 2026)

*A prep-reading map for the current schedule. Primary reading is Larry Wasserman's* All of Statistics *(2004); for more mathematical depth, Casella & Berger's* Statistical Inference *(2002) is cited where it's actually relevant; intuition and in-class visuals are drawn from Grinstead & Snell's* Introduction to Probability*, Efron & Hastie's* Computer Age Statistical Inference *(CASI), Gelman, Hill & Vehtari's* Regression and Other Stories *(ROS), and David Spiegelhalter's* The Art of Statistics *— the last for in-class display, not assigned reading. Every chapter and figure number below was confirmed against an actual copy — the five already on hand as PDFs (Wasserman, Grinstead & Snell, CASI, Casella & Berger, and Penguin's own official sample of Spiegelhalter) were read directly; ROS was checked against its official public companion site (`avehtari.github.io/ROS-Examples`) rather than a local copy, since no local copy exists and the only one findable online was an unauthorized upload I deliberately did not use. Nothing here touches the site or the source repo; this is a planning document only, and it lives only in this `Understanding Uncertainty` folder — I checked the read-only `uu_fa26` source repo and confirmed no copy exists there.*

**How to read the table:** "Primary" and "Supporting" (both Wasserman) are the default assigned reading. "C&B (more rigor)" is optional, for anyone who wants the fuller derivation — Casella & Berger is a proof-heavy graduate text, not a substitute for Wasserman, and several of our topics simply aren't in it. "Intuition" points to a specific passage in Grinstead & Snell, CASI, or Spiegelhalter that explains the idea in plain language *before* the formalism — real sections of real books, described in my own words, not quoted at length. "Visual supplements" are specific, real figures from Grinstead & Snell, CASI, ROS, and Spiegelhalter, worth pulling up during class — Spiegelhalter's are marked **(sample)** where the figure is inside the free 45-page Penguin preview you can open right now, or **(full book)** where it's real but requires the actual book. "Notebook source" is the actual `uu_fa26` file the class is built from.

## Two honest gaps in Wasserman (and Casella & Berger) — one of them now filled

**Vectors and inner product (8/27) and data wrangling (8/25) aren't in any of these texts.** All of them assume linear algebra and basic data-handling as prerequisites rather than teaching them. The class notebooks are the primary source for both; a standard linear-algebra reference (Strang) would be a better second source than any of these if you want one.

**Survival function and hazard rate (9/17) isn't in Wasserman, and it isn't in Casella & Berger either** — I checked; neither has a survival-analysis chapter. It *is* a full chapter in CASI (Ch. 9, "Survival Analysis and the EM Algorithm," §9.1–9.4: life tables, Kaplan–Meier, the log-rank test, and proportional hazards), which is the real fix for this gap.

**KDE (9/15) is the mirror-image case**: Wasserman covers it (Ch. 20) but none of the other five sources do, since none of them is a density-estimation text.

## If you want a gentler on-ramp before Wasserman

The chapter guide already in `_Knowledge Base/textbooks/Understanding Uncertainty Prep/` notes that Wasserman's Part I (Chapters 1–5) covers roughly the same ground as **Grinstead & Snell**, Chapters 1, 2, and 4–9, at about a third of the pace and with much more derivation. For 9/1 and 9/22–10/1 specifically, Grinstead & Snell is worth having as a slower-paced backup for any student who finds Wasserman's compressed treatment too terse on a first pass.
