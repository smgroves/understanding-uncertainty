The Statistics Zoo

Statistics vocabulary makes simple ideas sound like separate species. Most of them aren't — a random variable, a PMF, a CDF, an eCDF, and the bootstrap are the same handful of moves, worn as different costumes. Each card below gives one concept a plain-English definition, one thing you can touch, and an honest list of what it's secretly the same as.

01

Random Variable

A random variable is just a rule that turns an outcome into a number. Flip a coin — "heads" and "tails" aren't numbers, but "heads = 1, tails = 0" is a random variable.

Method of moments is the older, simpler cousin: just match the sample's own mean and variance to the distribution's formulas for mean and variance, and solve. Law of Large Numbers

As you collect more data, your sample average settles down and converges to the true population average — reliably, not just by luck. This is the "weak law" (WLLN); draw more data and watch the path stop wandering.
